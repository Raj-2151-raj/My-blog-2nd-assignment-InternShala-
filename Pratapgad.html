Pratapgad Fort

Explore the History and Beauty of Pratapgad Fort

Pratapgad is a mountain fort located in Satara district, in the Western Indian state of Maharashtra. The fort is situated 24 kilometres from the Mahabaleshwar hill station. The fort is now a popular tourist destination.
The fort's historical significance is due to the Battle of Pratapgad, which took place here on 10 November 1659, between Chatrapati Shivaji Maharaj and Bijapur Sultanate general Afzal Khan. The fort was constructed on a hilltop, about 1,080 meters above sea level, offering stunning views of the surrounding landscapes. There are two main entrances to the fort, and the fort’s gates are protected by high walls. It is divided into two parts – the lower fort and the upper fort. At present, the lower fort houses the main entrance, while the upper fort contains temples, a modern statue of Shivaji Maharaj, and several buildings. The fort has several watch towers and bastions for providing strategic military advantages during its time of use.

26th March 2024: Trip to PRATAPGAD (Total Distance traveled: 120 km):

We left Pune by 7 AM. Without taking any pit stop, we reached Pratapgad fort base by 10 AM. NH-4 highway is now extended up to 6 lanes highway. Apart from a few patches between Wai Phata and Wai, the rest of the road is in good condition.

Limited car parking is available at the base. The Road on the left side of the parking goes to the steps of the Fort. After climbing a few steps, we reached Mahadarwaja (Main Entrance) which is built between two bastions. There are 2 small rooms which were used by guards. One of the Guardroom has a Cannon.

TEHAMI BURUJ


Take the right side route after crossing Mahadarwaja for Tehani Buruj (Watchtower). This is the famous bastion which is always shown in Pratapgad fort's photo. It was used to keep a close watch on the nearby valley. The watchtower offers you a beautiful breathtaking valley view. You can see Fort view from this bastion as the bastion lies on the lower part of the fort. After spending some time on the Bastion, we returned towards the entrance gate. There are steps which take you to the upper part of the Fort. You can see the Lake on your right side while climbing the steps. This Lake is known as Rahat Lake. There are a total of 4 lakes on the fort.


PRATAPGAD KA NAZARA


Lake at Pratapgad Fort


We went to the top of the fort first where the statue of Shivaji Maharaj is mounted on the horse with a sword in his right hand. The statue was unveiled by late Jawaharlal Nehru (Prime Minister). As per locals, this place was used to be a palace of Shivaji Maharaj. This point is the topmost point of the fort also known as Bale Killa.

We settled down here and had a good discussion on the fort history. Whenever we visit any fort, we love to read/discuss the fort and its history. It gives you a different perception to explore the fort. This was the Hill known as "Bhorpya" which was used as a watchtower. Shivaji Maharaj captured the place and ordered to construct the fort. The fort was built under Shivaji Maharaja's Prime Minister 'Moropant Pingle'. During Maratha emperor, the Prime Minister was recognized as Peshwa. The fort came into the limelight due to the Battle between Maharaj and Afzal Khan.

Afzal Khan was the strongest and most powerful warrior from the Adilshah emperor. He was ordered to capture or kill Shivaji Maharaj by Adilshah Sultan. He came along with 20000 cavalries, more than 15000 infantry, 1500 gun army, 15000 Khan's personal army, 100+ elephant base army, 1000+ camel base army, and 150+ canons for the battle (Count varies in different books). Shivaji Maharaj was in Pune at that time and he knew that defeating around Pune was not possible hence he moved to Pratapgad. Considering Pratapgad and nearby hill terrain, Afzal's most of the army was useless in battle. He settled down in Wai as he was aware of the Wai area.

Shivaji offered a meeting at the base of Pratapgad which was agreed by Afzal Khan. He agreed with one condition that the meeting will take place with few armed bodyguards and leaders without any weapons. The meeting was scheduled for 10 November 1659. Afzal Khan joined the meeting place with Katyar (Small sword) hidden under a cloth. Shivaji Maharaj was aware of the intention of Khan and he wore protective armor under his clothes. Also, Shivaji Maharaj carried the weapon Vagh Nakh (Weapon looks like Tiger claws made up of sharp iron) for self-defense.

During the meeting, Afzal gave a tight hug to Maharaj and struck him with a small sword. Shivaji Maharaj was saved by the armor. As a counter-attack, Maharaj attacked Khan with Wagh Nakh. Injured Khan ran out of the Tent (Meeting place) for help. Sayyad Banda (Khan's bodyguard) attacked Shivaji Maharaj but Jiva Mahala (Shivaji's Bodyguard) intervened and chopped Sayyad Banda's hand with the sword. This event is remembered in Marathi as "Hota Jiva Mhanun vachala Shiva" which means because of Jiva, Shiva lived.

Afzal Khan went in Palkhi (non-wheel Vehicle to carry the leader) but Palkhi lifters were attacked by Shivaji's bodyguard Sambhaji Kavji. He killed Afzal Khan. After the war, Shivaji Maharaj sent Afzal Khan's head to Rajgad to show it to Jijabai (Shivaji Maharaj's mother) as Shahaji Raje (Father of Shivaji Maharaj) was captive of Afzal Khan and Afzal played the main role to kill Sambhaji (Elder son of Shahaji Raje, Do not confuse with Sambhaji Maharaj as he was the son of Shivaji Maharaj. The same name was used in multiple generations).

After Afzal's death, Shivaji Maharaj gave a sign (By lighting cannons) to attack Afzal's army. Quick action by Shivaji's army and without Afzal Khan, his army failed to fight. Adilshah lost too many armies (more than 13000) which was a major setback to his emperor. Shivaji Maharaj won many weapons, cannons, horse, elephant, camel, stored grains, and money. All captured war prisoners were sent back to Bijapur with proper respect. This remarkable victory made Shivaji Maharaj a hero in the emperor and famous as a great mind warrior within India at that time. Shivaji also buried Afzal Khan as per Islamic customs and built his tomb near Pratapgarh, as per his philosophy of ‘once the enemy is dead, the enmity is dead too’.

We left the statue to explore the remaining part of the Fort. We visited Keshawraj temple which is located on Bale Killa. There is a small place opposite the temple which is known as Sadar. A place built for important meetings and discussions.

There is Hastakala Kendra shop for handicraft products. The view of Tehani Buruj (Watchtower bastion) is beautiful. After a quick visit to the shop, we took a lunch break at Hotel Rasoi. It is opposite the side of Bhavani temple entrance. You can have a tasty Maharashtrian meal here along with the fort view.
